Week 02: From Ideation to Building Solutions

Week 2 of ProtoSem was a transition from understanding concepts to applying them practically. The week focused on developing the ability to think creatively, structure ideas logically, understand processes, and finally convert a problem into a functional digital solution.

💡 1. Learning How to Ideate

The week began with learning how to ideate and develop meaningful ideas. Rather than immediately jumping into solutions, we were encouraged to understand the problem first, explore different possibilities, and generate multiple ideas.

This helped me understand that a good idea is not necessarily the most complicated one. A useful idea should address a real problem, have a clear purpose, and provide value to its users.

🧹 2. Understanding the Japanese 5S Method

We were introduced to the 5S methodology, a Japanese workplace organization method widely used to improve efficiency and maintain an organized working environment.

Seiri (Sort)

Remove unnecessary items and keep only what is required.

Seiton (Set in Order)

Arrange necessary items so that they are easy to find and use.

Seiso (Shine)

Keep the workplace clean and identify sources of problems.

Seiketsu (Standardize)

Create consistent practices and standards.

Shitsuke (Sustain)

Develop discipline to continuously follow the established practices.

What made this session particularly useful was the practical application of the method. We were able to understand how 5S can be implemented rather than learning it only as a theoretical concept.

Key Learnings:
  • Organization can directly improve productivity and efficiency.
  • Small improvements in the workplace can reduce time and effort.
  • Standardization helps maintain consistency.
  • Continuous improvement requires discipline and regular practice.
  • The principles of 5S can also be applied beyond companies, including in personal and academic environments.

🔄 3. Algorithms and Flowcharts

We then moved towards computational thinking by learning about algorithms and flowcharts.

An algorithm provides a step-by-step procedure for solving a problem, while a flowchart visually represents those steps using standardized symbols. We worked through practical examples to understand how a problem can be broken down into smaller, logical steps before attempting to solve it.

Key Learnings:
  • Breaking complex problems into smaller steps.

Phase 01

Week 04: Embedded Systems, Sensors, ESP32 and IoT

Week 4 of Protosem focused on understanding the connection between hardware, programming, sensors, embedded systems, and IoT. The week provided a practical learning experience where we moved from basic concepts to building and experimenting with functional electronic systems.

Embedded Systems and Arduino IDE

We began the week by learning about Embedded Systems and understanding how hardware and software work together to perform specific functions. We were introduced to the Arduino IDE, which is used to write, compile, and upload programs to Arduino boards.

As a hands-on activity, we created basic Arduino models in which an LED could be turned on, turned off, and made to blink according to programmed instructions. This helped us understand how code can interact with physical components and control their behaviour.

Understanding Sensors and Actuators

We then explored the concepts of Sensors and Actuators. We learned how sensors detect and collect information from the surrounding environment, while actuators perform an action based on instructions received from a control system.

To strengthen our understanding, we documented a particular sensor and actuator, studying their working principles, components, connections, and practical implementation.

Working with Sensors using Arduino

After understanding the basics, we moved into practical implementation using Arduino. We worked with a Temperature and Humidity Sensor to understand how environmental conditions can be detected and converted into data that can be read by a microcontroller.

We also worked with an IR Sensor, learning how infrared signals can be used for detection and how the sensor communicates with the Arduino to produce a specific output.

These activities gave us practical experience in connecting sensors, writing programs, uploading code, and observing real-time outputs.

Introduction to ESP32

The next stage introduced us to the ESP32, a microcontroller with built-in wireless connectivity and a wide range of applications. We explored its basic features, pins, connectivity, and how it can be interfaced with different electronic components and sensors.

Using the ESP32, we created an Ultrasonic Sensor project, which helped us understand how ultrasonic signals can be used to measure distance and how the sensor communicates with the microcontroller.

We also developed a Joystick Game using ESP32, combining hardware connections, programming logic, user input, and output to create an interactive application.

Introduction to IoT

Then we were introduced to Internet of Things (IoT) and gained a brief understanding of its applications and different layers. We learned how IoT enables physical devices to collect, communicate, and exchange data through networks, creating connected and remotely controllable systems.

We then moved from the concepts to the practical implementation of IoT. We connected a circuit through Arduino to Wi-Fi and experimented with controlling a light through the connected system. We were able to perform functions such as turning the light ON and OFF and changing its colours through certain commands , etc...

This activity helped us understand how a physical device can be connected to the internet and controlled through a digital system, giving us a practical introduction to the concept of connected and smart devices.

Key Learnings

  • Understanding Embedded Systems, Arduino, microprocessors and microcontrollers.
  • Working with Arduino IDE and programming hardware components.
  • Understanding sensors, actuators and real-time data.
  • Interfacing Temperature & Humidity, IR and Ultrasonic Sensors.
  • Exploring ESP32 and its wireless connectivity.
  • Developing interactive projects using joystick input.
  • Understanding the fundamentals, layers and applications of IoT.
  • Understanding how hardware, software and connectivity work together to create smart systems.

Reflection

Week 4 gave me a broader understanding of how technology moves from basic electronic circuits to connected and interactive systems. Starting with Arduino and simple LED experiments, we progressed to sensors, ESP32, interactive projects, and finally IoT-based Wi-Fi connectivity.

The practical activities helped me understand that programming is not limited to software alone—it can be used to sense the environment, control physical components, communicate through networks, and create connected solutions. This week has given me valuable hands-on exposure to embedded systems, sensors, microcontrollers, ESP32, IoT and so on.
