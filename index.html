Phase 01

Week 02: From Ideation to Building Solutions

Week 2 of ProtoSem was a transition from understanding concepts to applying them practically. The week focused on developing the ability to think creatively, structure ideas logically, understand processes, and finally convert a problem into a functional digital solution.

💡 1. Learning How to Ideate

The week began with learning how to ideate and develop meaningful ideas. Rather than immediately jumping into solutions, we were encouraged to understand the problem first, explore different possibilities, and generate multiple ideas.

This helped me understand that a good idea is not necessarily the most complicated one. A useful idea should address a real problem, have a clear purpose, and provide value to its users.

🧹 2. Understanding the Japanese 5S Method

We were introduced to the 5S methodology, a Japanese workplace organization method widely used to improve efficiency and maintain an organized working environment.

Seiri (Sort)

Remove unnecessary items and keep only what is required.

Seiton (Set in Order)

Arrange necessary items so that they are easy to find and use.

Seiso (Shine)

Keep the workplace clean and identify sources of problems.

Seiketsu (Standardize)

Create consistent practices and standards.

Shitsuke (Sustain)

Develop discipline to continuously follow the established practices.

What made this session particularly useful was the practical application of the method. We were able to understand how 5S can be implemented rather than learning it only as a theoretical concept.

Key Learnings:
  • Organization can directly improve productivity and efficiency.
  • Small improvements in the workplace can reduce time and effort.
  • Standardization helps maintain consistency.
  • Continuous improvement requires discipline and regular practice.
  • The principles of 5S can also be applied beyond companies, including in personal and academic environments.

🔄 3. Algorithms and Flowcharts

We then moved towards computational thinking by learning about algorithms and flowcharts.

An algorithm provides a step-by-step procedure for solving a problem, while a flowchart visually represents those steps using standardized symbols. We worked through practical examples to understand how a problem can be broken down into smaller, logical steps before attempting to solve it.

Key Learnings:
  • Breaking complex problems into smaller steps.
  • Writing instructions in a clear and logical sequence.
  • Identifying decisions and possible outcomes.
  • Understanding how flowcharts visually represent processes.
  • Developing structured and analytical thinking.
  • Identifying errors or missing steps before implementing a solution.

🐱 4. Exploring Scratch and Visual Programming

We were also introduced to Scratch, a visual programming platform that allows users to create interactive animations and programs using block-based coding.

Through Scratch, we learned how different instructions can be connected to create animations, interactions, and simple applications.

Although Scratch uses visual blocks instead of traditional programming syntax, it helped us understand fundamental programming concepts such as:

Sequence Events Conditions Loops User interaction Logical thinking

This session became particularly relevant because the concepts we learned through Scratch helped us prepare for the next day's challenge of creating an application.

🚀 5. From Problem Identification to a Functional App

We were given the challenge of identifying a problem statement and developing a fully functional application to address it.

Instead of being given a predefined problem and solution, we had to think independently about:

Problem Idea Solution Features Application

This required us to apply several of the concepts learned throughout the week. We first identified a problem that could be addressed through an application. We then thought about how the application could solve the problem, what features would be required, and how the user would interact with it.

The process involved converting our idea into a structured solution and then implementing it as a functional app.

What I Learned From Building the App:
  • Problem-solving: Identifying pain points and designing solutions.
  • Logical thinking: Structuring the app's flow step-by-step.
  • Creativity: Designing user-friendly interfaces.
  • Implementation: Translating ideas into functional software.
  • Teamwork and collaboration: Working effectively with peers.
  • Time management: Delivering a functional product within a deadline.

Week 2 helped me understand that innovation is not just about having an idea; it is about understanding a problem, structuring the solution, and actually implementing it.
